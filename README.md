# Novelty Assessment & Literature Review for "MF-JEPA" (Multi-Facet JEPA)

## TL;DR
- The **core mechanism** of MF-JEPA — augmenting a JEPA encoder with additional complementary prediction "facets" — is **not new in the abstract**: MC-JEPA (2023), C-JEPA (NeurIPS 2024), and "Why and How Auxiliary Tasks Improve JEPA Representations" (2025) all add auxiliary objectives to JEPA. But the **specific synthesis** MF-JEPA proposes — predicting hand-crafted Gabor structural descriptors at BOTH visible (A1) and masked (A2) patches through the JEPA predictor, plus asymmetric context-only domain-shift corruption, applied to palmprint biometrics — appears genuinely novel with no exact prior match.
- The two closest conceptual precedents are **MaskFeat** (predict HOG hand-crafted features at masked patches) and **Albuquerque et al. 2020** (introduces a Gabor-filter-response prediction pretext task combined with rotation + DeepCluster for domain generalization). MF-JEPA effectively fuses these ideas *inside* JEPA's latent-prediction framework, which neither did — this is the defensible novelty.
- **In the palmprint domain the idea is clearly novel**: a dedicated search confirmed **no JEPA/joint-embedding-predictive architecture has yet been applied to palmprint or hand biometrics**. Frame the contribution as (a) a JEPA + hand-crafted-structural-target multi-facet framework and (b) its first application to cross-domain/open-set palmprint recognition — not as the invention of multi-task JEPA per se.

## Key Findings

**1. Multi-task/auxiliary JEPA already exists — position MF-JEPA as a specific instantiation, not a first.** MC-JEPA (Bardes, Ponce, LeCun, 2023, arXiv:2307.12698) states verbatim: "We hope that MC-JEPA will be a first step towards self-supervised learning approaches that are based on multi-task learning and joint-embedding architectures," jointly learning "optical flow and content features within a shared encoder." C-JEPA (Mo et al., NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2410.19560) adds VICReg variance/covariance terms to I-JEPA to prevent collapse. The 2025 paper "Why and How Auxiliary Tasks Improve JEPA Representations" (arXiv:2509.12249) gives a theoretical account (a "No Unhealthy Representation Collapse" theorem) of why an auxiliary regression head anchors which distinctions the representation must preserve. Reviewers WILL know that "add auxiliary objectives to JEPA" is established; MF-JEPA's novelty must live in the *choice* of facet (hand-crafted Gabor structure) and the *dual visible+masked prediction* design.

**2. Predicting hand-crafted descriptors as SSL targets is well established — MaskFeat is the key precedent.** MaskFeat (Wei, Fan, Xie, Wu, Yuille, Feichtenhofer, CVPR 2022, arXiv:2112.09133) masks patches and predicts their HOG descriptor. Its abstract states verbatim: "We study five different types of features and find Histograms of Oriented Gradients (HOG), a hand-crafted feature descriptor, works particularly well in terms of both performance and efficiency" (reaching 86.7% with MViTv2-L on Kinetics-400). This is the strongest precedent for MF-JEPA's Gabor-target facet and a critical citation. The difference: MaskFeat predicts HOG only at *masked* regions via an MAE-like decoder; MF-JEPA predicts Gabor energy at *both* visible (A1) and masked (A2) patches, with the appearance target being JEPA latent (not pixel/decoder). The "predict hand-crafted feature at masked location" idea is therefore not novel; the "also predict at visible patches + do it alongside JEPA latent prediction" framing is the differentiator.

**3. Gabor-as-SSL-target has a direct precedent in the domain-generalization literature — this is the single most important paper for the novelty assessment.** Albuquerque, Naik, Li, Keskar & Socher, "Improving out-of-distribution generalization via multi-task self-supervised pretraining" (2020, arXiv:2003.13525), states verbatim: "We introduce a new self-supervised pretext task of predicting responses to Gabor filter banks and demonstrate that multi-task learning of compatible pretext tasks improves domain generalization performance as compared to training individual tasks alone" (combined with DeepCluster + Rotation). This matters because it (a) uses Gabor responses as a prediction target and (b) frames it as multi-task SSL for robustness/OOD — very close to MF-JEPA's motivation. MF-JEPA's distinction is doing this within JEPA's latent-predictive framework (not CNN pretext-classification) and at masked patches via the predictor. The domain-generalization survey by Zhou et al. (2021, arXiv:2103.02503) also documents a three-pretext-task combo of "reconstructing the Gabor filter's response, predicting rotations, and predicting feature cluster assignments," confirming Gabor-response prediction is a known auxiliary SSL signal.

**4. Corruption-robust / frequency-aware SSL is a mature area.** MFM (Masked Frequency Modeling, Xie et al., ICLR 2023, arXiv:2206.07706) shows corruption-based tasks (super-resolution, deblur, denoise, frequency masking) are generally MORE robust than spatial MIM (MAE/SimMIM) — directly relevant to MF-JEPA's corruption-robustness motivation. MF-JEPA's approach of applying color-temperature/gamma/channel-mix/desaturation/blur/noise/vignette corruption ONLY to context patches (asymmetric corruption) is a reasonable, less-explored variant and a defensible novelty point, but should be positioned relative to MFM and the general "corrupt-and-predict" paradigm.

**5. Combining multiple heterogeneous pretext tasks is a classic, well-cited idea.** Doersch & Zisserman, "Multi-task Self-Supervised Visual Learning" (ICCV 2017, arXiv:1708.07860), states verbatim that "combining tasks—even via a naïve multi-head architecture—always improves performance" (evaluated with ResNet-101 across ImageNet classification, PASCAL VOC detection, and NYU depth prediction). This is the canonical multi-pretext-task reference; MF-JEPA's "multi-facet" framing is conceptually descended from it. Older single pretext tasks (rotation, jigsaw, colorization, context prediction) provide the historical arc.

**6. Palmprint SSL exists but is thin, and JEPA-for-palmprint does NOT exist.** Prior SSL palmprint work exists (Fan & Han, SIViP 2024, contextual-prediction SSL). Cross-domain/single-source-domain-generalization palmprint work exists (Jia et al., Pattern Recognition 2025). But a targeted search confirmed **no JEPA/joint-embedding-predictive architecture has been applied to palmprint or hand biometrics** — a legitimate, defensible novelty claim for the application contribution (recheck immediately before submission, since absence-of-evidence is not absolute proof).

## Details / Grouped Reference List

### A. JEPA family (foundational — cite all)
- **I-JEPA** — Assran, Duval, Misra, Bojanowski, Vincent, Rabbat, LeCun, Ballas. "Self-Supervised Learning from Images with a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture." CVPR 2023 (arXiv:2301.08243). The base method MF-JEPA extends; predicts masked target-block latent representations from a context block using a ViT context encoder, EMA target encoder, and narrow-ViT predictor.
- **V-JEPA** — Bardes et al. "V-JEPA: Latent Video Prediction for Visual Representation Learning." 2024 (OpenReview WFYbBOEOtv). Extends JEPA to spatiotemporal masked latent prediction in video (82.1% Kinetics-400, 71.2% SSv2 frozen-eval).
- **V-JEPA 2** — Assran et al. "V-JEPA 2: Self-Supervised Video Models Enable Understanding, Prediction and Planning." 2025 (arXiv:2506.09985). Per the abstract, pre-trained "on a video and image dataset comprising over 1 million hours of internet video"; the encoder "scales from 300 million parameters (ViT-L) to over 1 billion parameters (ViT-g)," reaching 77.3 top-1 on Something-Something v2 and 39.7 recall-at-5 on Epic-Kitchens-100, plus an action-conditioned world-model variant (V-JEPA 2-AC).
- **V-JEPA 2.1** — Mur-Labadia et al. 2026 (arXiv:2603.14482). Introduces, verbatim, a "Dense Predictive Loss, a masking-based self-supervision objective where all tokens (both visible/context and masked tokens) contribute to the self-supervised training loss" (scaled to 2B parameters, VisionMix-163M). This is a direct, independent validation of MF-JEPA's core idea of also predicting at visible patches (its A1 facet) — a must-cite parallel.
- **LeCun (2022)** — "A Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence." The JEPA position paper defining the architecture family and its energy-based-model framing.
- **data2vec / data2vec 2.0** — Baevski et al. (2022). Predicts latent representations of masked inputs across modalities using an EMA teacher; the closest conceptual relative of JEPA, always cited alongside.
- **LeJEPA** — Balestriero & LeCun (2025). Adds Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization (SIGReg), removing EMA-teacher reliance; relevant for the collapse-prevention discussion.

### B. Non-contrastive / variance-covariance SSL (baselines & regularization)
- **VICReg** — Bardes, Ponce, LeCun. ICLR 2022 (arXiv:2105.04906). Variance-Invariance-Covariance Regularization; a stated MF-JEPA baseline and the regularizer used inside I-JEPA and C-JEPA.
- **Barlow Twins** — Zbontar, Jing, Misra, LeCun, Deny. ICML 2021 (arXiv:2103.03230). Redundancy-reduction via a cross-correlation-to-identity objective; no negatives, robust to small batch size.
- **BYOL** — Grill et al. NeurIPS 2020. EMA target + predictor, no negatives; the anti-collapse mechanism I-JEPA inherits.
- **SimSiam** — Chen & He. CVPR 2021. Stop-gradient asymmetry, no negatives/momentum.

### C. Multi-task / multi-pretext-task SSL (the "multi-facet" precedents)
- **MC-JEPA** — Bardes, Ponce, LeCun. 2023 (arXiv:2307.12698). **Closest JEPA-specific precedent**: multi-task JEPA combining content features + optical flow in a shared encoder with VICReg. Must-cite and must-distinguish (its auxiliary task is optical flow, not a hand-crafted structural descriptor).
- **C-JEPA (Contrastive-JEPA)** — Mo et al. NeurIPS 2024 (arXiv:2410.19560). Integrates VICReg variance/invariance/covariance into I-JEPA to prevent entire collapse and improve mean-invariance; faster convergence than I-JEPA on ImageNet-1K. Second-closest JEPA precedent.
- **"Why and How Auxiliary Tasks Improve JEPA Representations"** — Yu et al. 2025 (arXiv:2509.12249). Theory of auxiliary heads in JEPA; the "No Unhealthy Representation Collapse" theorem directly justifies MF-JEPA's design and should anchor the theoretical motivation.
- **Doersch & Zisserman** — "Multi-task Self-Supervised Visual Learning." ICCV 2017 (arXiv:1708.07860). Canonical multi-pretext-task paper (combining relative position, colorization, exemplar, motion segmentation).
- **Classic single pretext tasks** — Gidaris, Singh, Komodakis (rotation prediction, ICLR 2018); Noroozi & Favaro (jigsaw, ECCV 2016); Zhang, Isola, Efros (colorization, ECCV 2016); Doersch, Gupta, Efros (context prediction, ICCV 2015). Historical grounding for "multiple complementary pretext signals."

### D. Hand-crafted features (Gabor/HOG/frequency) as deep/SSL targets
- **MaskFeat** — Wei, Fan, Xie, Wu, Yuille, Feichtenhofer. CVPR 2022 (arXiv:2112.09133). **Strongest hand-crafted-target precedent**: masks patches and predicts HOG descriptors; central to MF-JEPA's novelty argument.
- **Albuquerque, Naik, Li, Keskar, Socher** — "Improving out-of-distribution generalization via multi-task self-supervised pretraining." 2020 (arXiv:2003.13525). **Most important single precedent**: introduces a Gabor-filter-bank-response prediction pretext task, combined multi-task with rotation + DeepCluster, for domain generalization.
- **Masked Frequency Modeling (MFM)** — Xie, Li, Zhan, Liu, Ong, Loy. ICLR 2023 (arXiv:2206.07706). Frequency-domain masked prediction; shows corruption tasks are more robust than spatial MIM. Anchors the frequency/robustness discussion.
- **Learning Gabor Texture Features for Fine-Grained Recognition** — Zhu et al. ICCV 2023 (arXiv:2308.05396). A Gabor texture branch complementary to CNN features; supports the "Gabor captures complementary local/multi-frequency structure" premise.
- **GaborNet** — Alekseev & Bobe (2019, arXiv:1904.13204). Gabor-constrained learnable conv layer; grounds Gabor-in-deep-nets.
- **Survey**: "Masked Modeling for Self-supervised Representation Learning on Vision and Beyond" (arXiv:2401.00897). Catalogs MIM auxiliary targets including HOG (MaskFeat, FastMIM), Fourier-domain (Ge2AE, A2MIM), and position (DILEMMA, MP3) — useful to situate MF-JEPA within the target-diversity landscape.

### E. Masked image modeling (context for latent-vs-pixel prediction)
- **MAE** — He et al. CVPR 2022. Pixel-reconstruction masked autoencoder; the generative counterpoint to JEPA.
- **BEiT** — Bao et al. ICLR 2022. Masked visual-token (dVAE) prediction.
- **SimMIM** — Xie et al. CVPR 2022. Simple pixel-regression MIM with a lightweight head.

### F. Robustness / domain-generalization SSL
- **Albuquerque et al. 2020** (arXiv:2003.13525) — doubles as the key robustness-SSL + Gabor reference.
- **Domain Generalization: A Survey** — Zhou, Liu, Qiao, Xiang, Loy (arXiv:2103.02503). Documents multi-pretext-task DG, including the Gabor-response + rotation + cluster-prediction combination.
- **MFM** (Xie et al., ICLR 2023) — robustness-oriented corruption prediction.
- **Test-Time Training** — Sun et al. (2019/2020, arXiv:1909.13231). SSL auxiliary task (rotation) for distribution-shift robustness.

### G. Palmprint / hand biometrics (application domain)
- **Fan & Han** — "Deep palmprint recognition algorithm based on self-supervised learning and uncertainty loss." Signal, Image and Video Processing 18(5):4661–4673, 2024 (DOI 10.1007/s11760-024-03104-5). Contextual-prediction SSL pretraining + homoscedastic-uncertainty loss; closest palmprint-SSL precedent (validated on IITD, CASIA, PolyU).
- **Jia, Dong, Lai, Teoh et al.** — "Single source domain generalization for palm biometrics." Pattern Recognition 165:111620, 2025 (PII S0031320325002808; code: PalmRSS). Reframes SSDG as multi-source DG via source partitioning; Fourier Alignment Transform + Histogram Matching + CCNet + adversarial training for cross-spectral/cross-device generalization (PolyU, Tongji, IITD). Key cross-domain palmprint reference.
- **Gao, Yang, Jia, Leng, Zhang, Teoh** — "Deep Learning in Palmprint Recognition—A Comprehensive Survey." arXiv:2501.01166, 2025 (also IEEE Transactions on Systems, Man, and Cybernetics: Systems). Survey covering ROI segmentation, feature extraction, open-set, and security/privacy.
- **CCNet** — Yang, Huangfu, Leng, Zhang, Teoh, Zhang. "Comprehensive Competition Mechanism in Palmprint Recognition." IEEE TIFS 18:5160–5170, 2023 (DOI 10.1109/TIFS.2023.3306104). Standard SOTA baseline (spatial + channel competition over multi-order textures).
- **CO3Net** — Yang et al. "CO3Net: Coordinate-Aware Contrastive Competitive Neural Network for Palmprint Recognition." IEEE TIM 72, 2023 (DOI 10.1109/TIM.2023.3276506). Learnable-Gabor branches + coordinate attention + contrastive loss — **notably already fuses contrastive SSL with Gabor filtering in palmprint**, so distinguish carefully (it uses learnable Gabor conv layers, not Gabor descriptors as prediction targets).
- **CompNet** — Liang, Yang, Lu, Zhang. "CompNet: Competitive Neural Network for Palmprint Recognition Using Learnable Gabor Kernels." IEEE SPL 28:1739–1743, 2021. Learnable-Gabor predecessor to CO3Net/CCNet.
- **PalmNet** — Genovese, Piuri, Plataniotis, Scotti. "PalmNet: Gabor-PCA Convolutional Networks for Touchless Palmprint Recognition." IEEE TIFS 14(12):3160–3174, 2019 (DOI 10.1109/TIFS.2019.2911165). Gabor-PCA unsupervised baseline.
- **Open-set** — Zhong & Zhu. "Centralized large margin cosine loss for open-set deep palmprint recognition." IEEE TCSVT 30(6):1559–1568, 2020 (DOI 10.1109/TCSVT.2019.2904283).
- **Datasets** — PolyU multispectral (Zhang et al., IEEE TIM 59(2):480–490, 2010); IITD contactless (Kumar); Tongji contactless (Zhang et al., Pattern Recognition 69:199–212, 2017); CASIA-MS multispectral (CASIA/Institute of Automation).
- **Very recent (2026 preprints — flag as not peer-reviewed):** "PalmBridge: A Plug-and-Play Feature Alignment Framework for Open-Set Palmprint Verification" (arXiv:2601.20351); "X-Palm: Paired Multispectral-to-Smartphone Dataset for Cross-Domain Palmprint Authentication" (arXiv:2606.08437).

## Recommendations

**Framing (do this):**
1. **Do NOT claim "first multi-task/auxiliary JEPA."** Claim instead: "a multi-facet JEPA that predicts hand-crafted structural (Gabor) descriptors at both visible and masked patches, with asymmetric context-only corruption, and its first application to cross-domain/open-set palmprint recognition." Honest and defensible.
2. **Explicitly distinguish from the three closest works** in related work: MC-JEPA (auxiliary task = optical flow, not hand-crafted structure), MaskFeat (HOG at masked patches only, MAE-style decoder not latent JEPA), and Albuquerque et al. 2020 (Gabor-response prediction but CNN pretext-classification, not JEPA latent prediction). Add a comparison table with columns: *facet/target type · prediction space (pixel/feature/latent) · visible-vs-masked · robustness mechanism · application domain* — this makes novelty crisp at a glance.
3. **Lead the theoretical justification with Yu et al. 2025** ("auxiliary tasks anchor which distinctions the representation preserves"), converting your empirical design into a principled one, and cite C-JEPA/VICReg for the collapse-prevention rationale.
4. **Position the A1 (visible-patch) Gabor prediction against V-JEPA 2.1's Dense Predictive Loss** (all tokens contribute to the loss), which independently validates predicting at visible tokens and pre-empts a "why predict at visible patches?" reviewer objection.
5. **Distinguish carefully from CO3Net/CompNet** in the palmprint section: those use learnable Gabor *convolution kernels* as feature extractors; MF-JEPA uses fixed Gabor *descriptors as prediction targets*. This is a subtle but important difference reviewers in the biometrics community will probe.

**Experiments (to substantiate the combinatorial novelty claim):**
6. Add **ablations isolating each facet** (JEPA-only → +A1 → +A2 → +corruption), mirroring the multi-task ablation methodology of Doersch & Zisserman and Albuquerque et al. The multi-facet contribution must be *measurable*, since the claim is synthesis, not atomic invention.
7. Benchmark against a **MaskFeat-style HOG target** and a **Gabor-at-masked-only** variant to prove the visible+masked dual prediction and the Gabor (vs HOG) choice each add value.
8. For palmprint, compare against **CCNet / CO3Net / CompNet** (SOTA baselines) and evaluate cross-spectral/cross-dataset (PolyU↔Tongji↔IITD) and open-set protocols, citing Jia et al. 2025 for the SSDG protocol and Zhong & Zhu 2020 for the open-set protocol.

**Thresholds that would change these recommendations:**
9. If a pre-submission recheck finds any JEPA-palmprint paper, downgrade the application-novelty claim to "among the first." If any paper predicts Gabor descriptors *inside* a JEPA predictor, the core-novelty claim collapses to incremental — search arXiv for "JEPA Gabor," "JEPA hand-crafted target," and "JEPA texture" right before submitting.
10. If ablations show +A1/+A2/+corruption do NOT improve over JEPA-only, the paper's central thesis is unsupported and should be reframed around whichever single facet delivers gains.

## Caveats
- **Absence-of-evidence:** The "no JEPA for palmprint/hand biometrics" and "no Gabor-descriptor-target inside a JEPA predictor" claims are based on web/arXiv search as of August 2026 — strong but not absolute. Recheck immediately before submission.
- **Preprints:** Several closely related items — V-JEPA 2.1 (2026, arXiv:2603.14482), "Why and How Auxiliary Tasks Improve JEPA Representations" (2025), and palmprint works PalmBridge/X-Palm (2026) — are arXiv preprints not yet peer-reviewed; flag them as such.
- **Source hygiene:** Blog/aggregator pages (Emergent Mind, Medium, Turing Post) were used only for navigation; every primary claim traces to arXiv/CVF/IEEE/publisher pages, and the six most load-bearing claims were verified against verbatim source quotes.
- **Novelty is combinatorial, not atomic:** every ingredient (JEPA, auxiliary tasks, Gabor/HOG targets, corruption-robust SSL, palmprint SSL) has prior art. The contribution is the specific synthesis plus the new application domain. Reviewers will accept this framing only if the ablations demonstrate the synthesis outperforms its parts and the closest three precedents (MC-JEPA, MaskFeat, Albuquerque et al. 2020) are explicitly distinguished.
